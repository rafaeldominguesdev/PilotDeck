chapter 08 of 08 · team patterns

Three roles, one queue, no orphans

A card knows who asked for it, who executed it and who it returns to. Those three are rarely the same, and keeping them distinct is what lets a mixed human and agent team run without a standup.

01 · roles

Who asked, who ran it, who checks

RoleWho it usually isWhat it changes
Origin The human who wrote the card, or the agent that filed it during other work. Tells you whose intent this is when the contract turns out ambiguous.
Executor The CLI and model that claimed it, recorded at claim time. Explains the result. A swap away from the planned harness is on the card.
Returns to A named human, another agent, or the workspace queue. Decides where the card lands when it is delivered, so nothing waits on nobody.

The person who delegates is not always the person who checks. Set returns to deliberately: the reviewer should be whoever can actually run the How to confirm steps, which is often the person who felt the original pain.

02 · the queue

A review queue that stays short

  • Filter by awaiting my review and work it daily. A queue reviewed weekly stops being a queue and becomes a pile.
  • Cap work in progress. More cards in flight than reviewers is how a team ends up with a Done column nobody trusts.
  • Route to the workspace queue when nobody is obvious. That is better than assigning a reviewer who will not look, and anyone can pick it up.
  • Reopen instead of fixing it yourself. Fixing an agent's delivery by hand hides the failure from the numbers; the reopened rate is a signal you want to keep clean.
03 · missions

Missions carry the context a card cannot

A mission is a group of cards with an objective and context written once. Every briefing for a card in that mission carries it, so an agent picking up card nine knows what the other eight were for without you repeating yourself.

Missions are also the unit that most often deserves a price. "What did this feature cost?" is answered by grouping insights by mission, and that number is far more useful than a monthly total.

04 · rfcs

Decide as a card, then execute as cards

Work you cannot yet specify is not a feature card. Make it an rfc card whose deliverable is a document: the options, the tradeoffs, the recommendation. Its How to confirm is about the document, not about code, for example "the RFC names at least two rejected options and why".

When you validate the RFC, the execution cards it proposes get created and carry the decision with them. Design to execution stays traceable, and the expensive model that wrote the RFC is not the model that has to do every rename afterwards.

05 · parallel agents

Several agents, one board

  • One token per agent and machine. Revocable individually, with last use visible, so an agent that misbehaves is one click.
  • Claims are atomic. A second claim on the same card comes back as already claimed, so two agents cannot silently do the same work.
  • Branches per card. The card id in the branch, the commit and the pull request title is what keeps parallel work from colliding.
  • Team mode for a card with slices. A card can carry sub-tasks when the work genuinely splits, and the board shows the progress across them.
06 · the loop, once more

Where the course lands

Create a contract. Let an agent claim it and report back with evidence and cost. Review with the script you wrote before the work existed. Change the policy when the numbers tell you to. That is the whole loop, and every chapter here was one of its moving parts.

Going further: the MCP surface, the typed errors and the manage flag are documented in docs/mcp.md, and the roadmap lives on our own PilotDeck board. Yes: agents build this board through this board.

Chapter checklist

  • Every card has a deliberate returns-to, not a default one
  • The review queue is worked daily and stays short
  • Related cards live under a mission with real context
  • Undecided work goes through an rfc card before execution cards exist
  • Each agent has its own revocable token
  • Card ids appear in branches, commits and pull request titles
