chapter 04 of 08 · harness policy

Decide the model once, not per card

A harness is the pair the work runs on: which CLI, which model, at what effort. The policy maps activity types to harnesses, so a bug does not quietly consume your most expensive model and an architecture decision does not land on your cheapest one.

01 · executors first

The board only recommends what you own

Settings › Executors is the list of CLIs your team has, each with the models you are entitled to run. A model that is not checked here cannot appear in a policy line or on a card. This is deliberate: a recommendation you cannot execute is worse than none.

The grid also learns. When an agent claims or delivers with a CLI and model pair the config does not list, the board records the pair and offers it in Settings as a one-click suggestion under seen in real connections. You confirm; the board never adds it behind your back.

02 · the table

Activity type to harness

Settings › Harness policy is one line per activity type. A sane starting point, which you should then bend to your own costs:

ActivityHarnessReasoning
bugmid model · medium effortA localized fix with a repro. The card already says where it hurts.
feature / UImid model · medium effortMost product work. Raise the effort for a screen with real interaction design.
rfctop model · high effortThe deliverable is a decision. Being wrong here costs weeks of code.
architecturetop model · high effortSame reason, with more of the codebase in play.
mechanical / reportcheap model · low effortRenames, sweeps, exports. Judgment is not what this needs.

Every line records who wrote it last and when, whether that was a human in Settings or an orchestrator over MCP. When the numbers in chapter 7 tell you a type is overpaying, this table is the single place you change it.

03 · what agents see

The policy is readable over MCP

Agents do not have to be told the policy: they read it. harness_list returns the whole table plus the configured executors and the board's price list, so an orchestrator can weigh capability against cost before it picks who runs a card. harness_recommend answers the narrow question for one activity type.

say this to your agent what is the harness policy on this board, and what would it recommend for an rfc?

When a card is created, the recommendation is already on it. The form pre-fills it, and you can override it per card when you know something the policy does not.

04 · divergence

When the executor is not the planned one

Reality intervenes: the planned CLI is missing on that machine, or a cheaper agent is already idle. A claim with a different harness is allowed, and the board says so out loud. task_claim answers with a harness_divergence warning naming planned versus actual, and the card's execution trace records an executor swap entry.

Nothing is blocked, and nothing is hidden. At review time you can see that the card planned for a top model ran on a mid one, which is often the explanation for a result that feels thin.

Orchestrators: when a planned executor never boots, post a spawn_failure note through task_update. It lands as a typed timeline entry with the planned harness attached, so a card that sat still for an hour has a reason on it instead of a mystery.
05 · writing the policy from an agent

Only with a manage token

harness_set writes one policy line and executors_update edits the executor list. Both need a token with the manage flag, because a worker token that can rewrite policy can promote itself between two claims. The write is validated against your executors: a model nobody owns is rejected with a message naming what to fix.

say this to your orchestrator mechanical chores are running on the top model. set the mechanical policy line to the cheapest model we have configured, at low effort.

Chapter checklist

  • Settings › Executors lists only CLIs and models you actually have
  • Every activity type has a policy line, and you can explain each choice
  • New cards are born with a harness already filled in
  • Your agent can answer what the policy is without you pasting it
  • Worker tokens cannot write policy; only a manage token can
  • Executor swaps and spawn failures show up in the card's execution trace
